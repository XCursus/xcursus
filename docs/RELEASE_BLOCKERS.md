# Release blockers for 1.0

XCursus 1.0 is declared only when every [exit criterion](ROADMAP.md#exit-criteria) is met with recorded evidence. The work of 1.0 Stabilization is implemented and tested (see [VERIFICATION](VERIFICATION.md)); the items below are what is still missing. Until they are closed the repository holds a **1.0 candidate**, a pre-release, with the packages at 0.6.0. State on 5 November 2026.

Priorities: **P1** prevents declaring 1.0 (a criterion without evidence, or a defect that blocks an intended use); **P2** must be closed or explicitly accepted before 1.0.

Evidence on runners. On 4 November 2026 the maintainer decided that the browser matrix, touch and screen reader criteria are met with GitHub's hosted runners instead of physical devices and manual checks: release builds of the browsers, Apple's iOS Simulator and Google's Android Emulator with real touch input, and VoiceOver and NVDA driven through Guidepup. Every piece of such evidence is labelled with what it is. The `Release checks` workflow (`.github/workflows/release-checks.yml`) produces it, on demand and on `release/**` branches; the runs cited below are on `release/1.0-checks`, run 41943371096 (commit `1a22be4`) unless stated.

## B4 · P1 · Screen reader: VoiceOver's announcement of a move

- **Evidence:** item 7 of the [checklist](#manual-checklist) through Guidepup, with every phrase heard kept in the results. NVDA 2026.3 with Google Chrome 156.0.8078.80 on Windows Server 2025: 6 of 6; after each move NVDA says the scene title ("Detail", "Conclusion"), the Text view is announced as a list of named items, the choices of a branch are reached and named, every Studio toolbar button and inspector field is named, and the delete, export and import dialogs are announced by name with focus returning to the opener. VoiceOver (macOS 15.8.2) with WebKit 27.2, Playwright's build of the engine of Safari (Safari under WebDriver suspends the session at the first key a screen reader sends): the same five checks pass.
- **Missing evidence:** VoiceOver's announcement of the scene title after a move. Through Guidepup VoiceOver's last phrase stays the focused button after every move, also with the live region given `role="status"` or made visible, so the automation cannot tell a missing announcement from one it does not report; the check is recorded as not verifiable.
- **Closing criterion:** on a Mac, VoiceOver (Command-F5) with Safari on the playground (`pnpm dev:playground`, `/core`): focus Player 1's **Next** and press it twice; VoiceOver says "Detail", then "Conclusion". Record macOS, Safari and the outcome in VERIFICATION. If it stays silent, the player's live region is fixed first.
- **Finding recorded (P3):** the choices of a branch are not announced when the player arrives at it; they are reached with Tab or the reader's navigation.

## Closed

- **B1 · Real Safari, two latest versions** (closed 5 November 2026). Safari 27.2 (20625.2.8.18.4), the latest version (released 14 September 2026, macOS 15 and later), on macOS 15.8.2, macOS 15 runner image 20261028.0489: items 1–5 of the [checklist](#manual-checklist) through safaridriver, 15 of 15 verifiable checks passed. On that runner Safari cannot read local files (the chooser accepts them, the page then gets `NotReadableError`) and refuses full screen (`InvalidStateError`): there file inputs are filled from the page and the exported HTML is served over http, recorded as stand-ins, and full screen is recorded as not verifiable. Safari 26.5.2 (19624.2.5.18.7), the previous version, on macOS 14.8.7 (development Mac, `pnpm test:safari`): 16 of 16 in three consecutive runs, with the real file picker, `file://` and full screen. In both: scenes framed by CSS 3D within 0.0001 px, keys only in the focused player, instant Text view and Reduce motion, Studio's map, scene editor, archive round trip and recovery, the impress.js import, the exported HTML and the presenter with its audience window.

- **B2 · Real Edge, Firefox and the previous Chrome** (closed 5 November 2026). On Ubuntu 24.04 runners every browser suite (104 tests, 0 failed, skipped or flaky) passed on Google Chrome 157.0.8103.38 and 156.0.8078.80 (Chrome for Testing, Google's build for automation of the stable releases) and on Microsoft Edge 156.0.4314.58 and 155.0.4283.62 (Microsoft's packages); release builds of Firefox 159.0.1 and 158.0.1 through geckodriver 0.37.1 passed items 1–5 of the checklist, 16 of 16 each, in every run since 4 November. The installed Google Chrome 156.0.8078.79 on the development Mac passed every suite as well.

- **B3 · Touch devices** (closed 5 November 2026, on simulated and emulated devices). Item 6 of the checklist with real touch input: Safari 26.5 in the iOS Simulator 26.5 on an iPhone 17 Pro (3 of 3) and an iPad Pro 13-inch (M5) (4 of 4, including Studio presenting), driven by safaridriver on the macOS 26 runner; Chrome 124.0.6367.219, the Chrome of the Android 15 (API 35) emulator image, with the Pixel 7 (3 of 3) and Pixel Tablet (4 of 4) profiles, through adb and DevTools touch events. Five defects were found this way and fixed, each with a regression check: swipes starting on scrollable scene content were cancelled by the browser; the outline overflowed narrow players whose control bar wraps and hid its end under the on-screen buttons; Safari on iOS reports a touch `pointerup` at (0, 0), which voided swipes; Studio's icon buttons and its faded presentation controls needed two taps on iPad. No physical device was used, and the emulator's Chrome is older than the current Chrome for Android.

- **B5 · Firefox painting of child scenes in front of large sections** (closed 4 November 2026). Cause: Gecko sorts the planes of a 3D scene by inserting them, in paint order, into a splitting tree; when a turned plane painted earlier cuts a very large plane, the fragments lose precision and child scenes 30 px in front of the section are drawn behind its background. Isolated with the stress deck to two reproductions of four scenes each (`tests/fixtures/paint`), and to a static page of eleven elements without any XCursus code that Firefox renders wrongly and Chromium correctly. Fix: in Gecko only, the player gives scene wrappers a paint order (`z-index`, the DOM is not reordered) in which the planes that cut the least area of the others come first and the scene being shown comes before all (`packages/player/src/paint.ts`); the 3D result is decided by depth as before. Evidence on the stress deck in Firefox: the 10 of 48 visits that showed missing or broken children (children of the two sections, the recursion, the sections themselves and a far overview) now match Chromium, the other 38 are unchanged and no seam appears on the scene shown; the unit test checks the order and its application, `tests/e2e/paint.spec.ts` checks in every engine that the children are drawn and fails in Firefox when the order is disabled. Chromium and WebKit get no `z-index` at all.

- **B6 · Performance gate on a controlled machine** (closed 5 November 2026). `pnpm benchmark --gate` on the development Mac, an Apple M3 Max (16 cores, 64 GiB, macOS 14.8.7), on mains power with no other application open, at commit `0a902a9` with a clean working tree, in Playwright's headless builds (WebKit through the macOS 14 runner): every budget met in Chromium 156.0.8078.4, Firefox 157.0 and WebKit 26.5 on the reference scenario (100 scenes × 10 text blocks of 100 characters, 1280×720, DPR 1, production build, 5 warm-ups + 20 runs). p95: validation 10.9 / 11.0 / 10.8 ms; mount-ready 328 / 427 / 227 ms; input → preview 41 / 34 / 30 ms; frame interval 16.7 / 16.7 / 18.0 ms over 10 transitions; no long task (observable in Chromium only); 50 mount/destroy cycles with zero owned resources in every engine and a heap delta of 0.05 MiB after GC (Chromium only). The first run of the gate failed in Firefox, mount-ready p95 5.1 s: since the fix of B5 the player recomputed the paint order every time a block renderer became ready, about a thousand times for the reference deck (the measurement on battery of 2 November predates B5). The order is now computed once per document, and the unit test counts it. On the runners, informative (Release checks run 41982361094): on macOS 15 (Apple M1 virtual machine, 3 cores, 7 GiB) with browser windows, Chromium 156 and WebKit 27.2 meet every budget and Firefox 157 every one but the frame interval (p95 51 ms; mount-ready 811 ms); on Ubuntu 24.04 (4 cores, headless) Chromium meets every budget, Firefox misses the frame interval only (50 ms; mount-ready 793 ms), and WebKit misses the frame interval (44 ms) while its input → preview measurement is incomplete (6 of 20 samples missing). Without a GPU, Firefox renders 3D in software (known issue in the [roadmap](ROADMAP.md#known-issues)).

- **B7 · Security reporting channel** (closed 4 November 2026): GitHub's private vulnerability reporting, the channel named in [SECURITY.md](../SECURITY.md), is enabled on `XCursus/xcursus` (`GET /repos/XCursus/xcursus/private-vulnerability-reporting` returns `{"enabled": true}`; it returned `false` on 2 November).

- **B8 · Linux CI run of the candidate** (closed 4 November 2026): the CI workflow passed on commit `326d32d` (run 41814077077, ubuntu-24.04): frozen install, typecheck, lint, format, 529 unit tests with coverage, build, boundaries, bundle, 104/104 package checks, 303 browser tests in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit with Playwright 1.64.1, and no tracked file modified by the checks.

## Not blocking, recorded

- **Development tooling advisory:** `pnpm audit` reports one moderate advisory (GHSA-g3hv-75wg-m249, `minimatch` < 11.0.1) reachable only through ESLint and typescript-eslint; `pnpm audit --prod` finds no known vulnerability in shipped code. Move the lint toolchain past it when compatible releases exist.

## Manual checklist

The checklist the evidence above follows; `scripts/checklist.mjs` (items 1–6) and `scripts/screen-reader.mjs` (item 7) carry it out, and it can still be run by hand. For each browser and device, record the version and the outcome of every line.

1. **Player** (`pnpm dev:playground`, `/core` and `/layout`): the first scene is framed; arrows, Page Up/Down and Space navigate only when the player has focus; Text view lists the path and navigates without motion; Reduce motion makes transitions instant.
2. **Studio** (`pnpm dev:studio`): open `examples/layout-canvas/layout-canvas.xcursus`; move a scene on the map and undo; edit a block in the scene editor; Save archive, reload, reopen it; Recovery offers nothing after saving.
3. **impress.js import**: Import impress.js with `examples/impress-import/deck.html` and its two images; the review lists the findings; Open candidate shows six scenes with both images.
4. **Exported HTML** (`pnpm xcursus export html examples/distribution-static/project.json --out deck.html`, opened from disk with the network off): Previous, Next and Full screen work; formulas, images and the theme font render.
5. **Presenter**: Presenter opens the audience window; notes stay in the console; navigation from either window stays in sync; with pop-ups blocked the single-window fallback appears.
6. **Touch** (tablets and phones): in the exported HTML a horizontal swipe moves to the next and previous visit, buttons are easy to hit, the outline is readable at 320 px width; Studio opens a project and presents it.
7. **Screen reader**: the player announces the scene title after each move and the choices of a branch; the outline is a navigable list; Studio's toolbar buttons, dialogs (delete, export, import) and inspector fields have names, and focus returns to the opener after a dialog closes.
